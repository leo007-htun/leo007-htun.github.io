About

I am a Research Assistant in the School of Computer Science and Mathematics at Liverpool John Moores University, working on applied AI and robotics. My research focuses on memory in language models: what information survives inside a model's hidden states, and how that internal state could become lasting memory for AI agents and robots. Alongside research I build systems end to end; my most recent is AON, a self-hosted AI agent published on PyPI and in the official MCP Registry.

Background. I studied engineering in the UK: a Level 3 Foundation Diploma in Engineering, an HND in Electrical and Electronic Engineering (Pearson, 2016–2018) and a B.Eng in Mechatronics at the University of Wolverhampton (2019–2020). I then worked as a ROS developer at ROM Robotics, designing robot models (URDF), working on localization (adaptive Monte Carlo) and building embedded Linux with the Yocto Project for robot controllers, before joining LJMU.

Research

I am interested in how models keep and use information over time. One line of work probes frozen transformers with preregistered experiments, matched nulls and held-out tests, to separate what a model genuinely retains from what only looks like memory. Another asks whether the fixed-size recurrent state of new hybrid models can be saved, combined and reloaded as long-term memory. Applied work brings AI onto robots and edge devices: emotion recognition on a social robot and real-time perception on a Raspberry Pi.

Key themes:

  • Memory in language models: Persistent State Memory in Hybrid Language Models (PhD proposal) and Recoverability of Token Identity in Delayed Transformer Hidden States
  • Social robotics: Pep-FER: Facial Emotion Recognition on a Pepper Social Robot
  • Edge AI and autonomy: Real-Time Autonomous Navigation on a Raspberry Pi (IEEE DeSE 2025)
  • Conversational AI: Design and Implementation of a Full Duplex Conversational Speech System
  • Self-hosted AI agents: AON, a private agent with memory, document search and an MCP server
